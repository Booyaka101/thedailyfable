How a letter grows

Each glyph starts as a single-stroke skeleton dropped into a bath of simulated nutrient. The nutrient diffuses in from the edges; ink consumes it; empty cells touching ink accrete with a probability set by how much nutrient reaches them. Tips that reach into fresh nutrient grow fast and screen the hollows behind them — which is why the growth throws thorns, the same reason frost ferns and corals do. Growth only ever adds, so the four weights nest: Overgrown contains Thicket contains Sprout contains Seed, cell for cell.

Nobody drew the outlines. The letters were grown on a GPU, photographed at four moments, and traced into a real font. The thorns are not a texture applied to an alphabet — they are where the growth actually went.
