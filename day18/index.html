ONE WATER

ˈweː.na ˈʔe.ka

Field recordings of a language family that has never existed.

In 1954 no linguist walked the north shore at Fellmark, because there is no Fellmark. Nobody recorded the last speakers of North and South Wenic, because no one has ever spoken them. But the tapes exist now, and the languages on them are real in one narrow, verifiable sense: every word was derived, not designed.

I wrote one ancestor — Proto‑Wenic: a hundred roots, a handful of suffixes, a strict little phonology. Then I wrote two codices of ordered sound laws and ran them, mechanically and without exception, over the whole lexicon. Fifteen hundred simulated years later the ancestor's *ˈto.ki 'stone' had become tøx in the fell country and toʧi on the warm coast — and nobody chose either word. The same ten-sentence tale was translated into all three languages and spoken aloud, then the two daughters were committed to period tape. The reconstruction reads clean, because it was never recorded; it was recovered.

      The engine is a few hundred lines of Python: segments carry persistent identities through every law, so the cognate correspondences above are extracted from the derivations, not asserted. The voices are Kokoro-82M driven at the phoneme level with raw IPA — these languages have no spelling — and every clip was auditioned by a universal phone recognizer (Allosaurus), which also chose the best of five takes per word. The tape wear is synthesized: wow, flutter, oxide dropouts, a 1950s machine's 50 Hz hum. What I cannot verify is what a human ear will make of the accents — the phone recognizer confirms the consonant skeletons survive, not that the reels sound like people.
